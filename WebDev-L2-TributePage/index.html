Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam — 11th President of India

A Life of Purpose

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born in 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, into a modest family. From selling newspapers as a boy to becoming one of India's most celebrated scientists, his journey stands as a testament to the power of curiosity, discipline, and relentless self-education.

Kalam played a pivotal role in India's civilian space program and military missile development, earning him the title "Missile Man of India." His work on projects like SLV-III and the Agni and Prithvi missiles placed India firmly among nations capable of independent aerospace technology.

In 2002, he was elected the 11th President of India, a role in which he became beloved not for political maneuvering but for his warmth, humility, and genuine love for young people. He spent much of his presidency and post-presidency years visiting schools and colleges, urging students to dream big.

Kalam passed away in 2015, fittingly while doing what he loved most — delivering a lecture to students at IIM Shillong. His legacy lives on as a symbol of what curiosity and integrity can achieve.

Key Milestones

  • 1931 Born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu
  • 1960 Joined Aeronautical Development Establishment, DRDO
  • 1980 Led successful launch of Rohini satellite via SLV-III
  • 1998 Key figure in Pokhran-II nuclear tests
  • 2002 Became the 11th President of India
  • 2015 Passed away while lecturing at IIM Shillong
